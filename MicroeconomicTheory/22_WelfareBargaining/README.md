# MWGWelfareBargaining

Formalization of Mas-Colell, Whinston and Green, *Microeconomic Theory*, Chapter 22, "Elements of
Welfare Economics and Axiomatic Bargaining", pp. 817-856. Highlights: Proposition 22.D.1
(Paretian, pairwise independent functionals are generated by a rational relation on `ℝ^I`, with
the footnote 17 chains), Propositions 22.D.2-22.D.3 (form (22.D.1); utilitarian form under
independent origins; dictatorship, also for functionals generated by any rational relation, by an
ultrafilter argument), Arrow's theorem by the route of p. 837 (Exercise 22.D.5, corrected),
Proposition 22.E.1 (Nash's characterization) with existence and uniqueness of the weighted Nash
solution, the egalitarian, utilitarian and Kalai-Smorodinsky solutions with their properties and
counterexamples, and the Shapley value with its axiomatic characterization (Exercises 22.F.2-
22.F.4, via Harsanyi dividends and Möbius inversion). Further: the Ramsey first-order conditions
by Mathlib's Lagrange multiplier theorem (Exercise 22.B.2), second-best and randomized utility
possibility sets, unproductive taxation (22.B); the constant elasticity family, its limits and
invariance (Example 22.C.4, Exercises 22.C.5, 22.D.7), leximin for any `I`, Harsanyi's theorem
(corrected), the compensation exercises (22.C); the classification of ordinally invariant social
welfare functions for `I = 2` (Exercise 22.D.8(d)), Example 22.D.1, and Debreu's separability
theorem of p. 838 for `I ≥ 3` (strictly increasing, solvable `W`; with Aczél's associativity
theorem as the one-dimensional step) with counterexamples for `I = 2` and for MWG's weak "increasing" (22.D); the independence of
the axioms of Proposition 22.E.1, the characterizations of the egalitarian (Exercise 22.E.5(a),
via a Hahn-Banach supporting half-space) and Kalai-Smorodinsky (Exercise 22.E.6) solutions,
strongly monotonic solutions as monotone-path solutions (Exercise 22.E.5(b), corrected),
consistency (Exercises 22.E.7-22.E.8), Rubinstein bargaining with concave utilities (Exercise
22.E.11) (22.E); the nucleolus (Exercise 22.F.5), Example 22.F.1 and the regulated firm and
Boiteux problems (Exercises 22.F.6-22.F.7) (22.F). The fresh-source audit passes **502 theorems
and 701 audited declarations across 30 modules**. Coverage, including the items left
OPEN, is in the [source map](docs/source-map.md); departures are in
[corrections](docs/corrections.md) (false printed claims refuted: the sign in Example 22.B.2,
Exercise 22.D.5's inheritance claim, Exercise 22.D.6(b), the Maskin remark of p. 838, the
transformation `h` of Example 22.C.4 for `ρ > 1`, Harsanyi's form in Exercise 22.C.3(d), Debreu's separability claim with MWG's weak
"increasing", and the curve through the origin of Exercise 22.E.5(b) without individual rationality).

This is a Lake project pinned to Lean and Mathlib `v4.34.0`. It imports (Lake `path`
requirements, no copies) MWG Chapter 21 (`MWGSocialChoice`: profiles, social welfare
functionals, Pareto, pairwise independence, dictatorship, `proposition_21_C_1`), MWG Chapter 10
(`MWGCompetitiveMarkets`: `Economy`, `utilityPossibilitySet`) and through it MWG Chapter 3
(`MWGClassicalDemand`: `example_3_C_1_not_representable`, `lexPref`), and Kreps Chapter 1
(`Rational`, `Strict`, `UtilityPref`, `proposition_1_3a`), and MWG Chapter 9 (`MWGDynamicGames`:
the alternating-offer game form (`BargainingProfile`, `path`, `Agrees`, `threshold`, deviations),
reused with utility payoffs for Exercise 22.E.11(c), and `example_9_AA_2` for the linear case), and MWG Chapter 5 (`MWGProduction`, reached through Chapter 10: Farkas's lemma
`farkas`, used for Harsanyi's theorem with any number of individuals). See [third-party notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
